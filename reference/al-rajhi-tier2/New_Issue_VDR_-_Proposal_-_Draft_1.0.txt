The New Issue VDR — Unique Selling Points

The Problem with the Status Quo

The international bond market's new issue dissemination infrastructure
is antiquated relative to the volumes and speeds involved:

- Outlook/Email is the default channel for term sheets and new issue
  alerts, but it is irreversible — once sent, a document cannot be
  retracted, corrected, or versioned. In a market where deal terms
  change rapidly during bookbuild, this is a meaningful operational and
  compliance risk.

- Bloomberg offers messaging and IB Chat as an alternative, but at
  significant cost. For banks (middle office) or investors who only need
  it for new issue alert dissemination, the Bloomberg terminal licence
  is a very expensive solution to a relatively narrow problem. It is
  also a closed, proprietary ecosystem.

- Taxonomy standardisation has historically been left entirely to the
  data vendors that is until recently — Whilst the ICMA taxonomy is
  agreed in principal the standard is not rigorously applied at source,
  at the point of origination by the bank. The result is fragmentation,
  reconciliation costs, and data inconsistency across vendor systems –
  it is an improving picture however.

- Pre-AI, there was no *easy* way to automate structured data extraction
  from free-form term sheet documents or chat rooms at source. Though
  complex algorithms can do this, human analysts at most vendors
  manually re-keyed the same data. This is expensive, slow, error-prone,
  and entirely unnecessary given current AI technology.

i) The Opportunity AI Creates — Automation at Source

AI changes the fundamental economics and architecture of new issue data
dissemination. For the first time, it is possible to automate structured
data extraction at the point of origination — the bank itself — rather
than leaving each downstream vendor to solve the same problem
independently.

This is the core insight: today, if there are ten data vendors in the
market, the same term sheet is being manually processed ten times, by
ten different teams, with ten slightly different results. The New Issue
VDR eliminates this redundancy entirely. The bank deposits the document
once. The AI extracts the data once, to a single agreed standard. Every
vendor receives the same clean, structured output simultaneously and
notably all vendors are included and synced into the entire process.

This is not incremental improvement — it is a structural shift in where
intelligence sits in the workflow.

ii) The Bank-Facing Product — A New Issue VDR with a Native Alert Thread

The product given to syndicate banks is intentionally simple. Banks do
not need to integrate new systems into their technology stack or change
their internal processes significantly. The interface is a New Issue
Alert Thread via a secure app/portal — conceptually familiar to any
banker who uses Bloomberg IB Chat or a group email chain, but
purpose-built for new issue dissemination.

Workflow……..

The bank's syndicate desk drops into this thread:

- The new issue alert (free text announcement)

- The term sheet (PDF, Word, or plain text)

- Any subsequent updates — revised size, tightened spread, final terms,
  pricing supplement

The VDR's AI layer immediately processes every item deposited into data
and suggested text (if required):

- Extracts all structured data fields against the ICMA bond data
  taxonomy (borrower, currency, size, tenor, coupon, spread, ratings,
  bookrunners, ISIN, settlement date, and all other standard fields)

- Populates the vendor database in real time.

- Pushes structured data to any subscribed vendors API or MCP.

- Flags version changes — if a second document is uploaded, the system
  detects what changed and updates only the affected fields, with a full
  audit trail and HITL controls.

The bank's workflow is reduced to a single action: drop content into the
thread. Everything downstream is automated.

iii) ICMA Taxonomy as the Universal Standard — Built In, Not Bolted On

One of the most commercially and structurally significant aspects of
this system is the decision to build the ICMA bond data taxonomy
directly into the AI extraction layer, rather than leaving taxonomy
decisions to individual vendors.

ICMA (the International Capital Market Association) is the recognised
self-regulatory body for the international bond markets and has
published data standards for primary market disclosure. By anchoring the
New Issue VDR to ICMA taxonomy:

- Every field extracted by the AI maps to an agreed, industry-recognised
  definition

- Vendors receiving the data do not need to reconcile different naming
  conventions

- Regulators and compliance teams have a clear, auditable standard
  against which disclosures can be assessed

- The system naturally aligns with emerging regulatory expectations
  around structured primary market data

The native messaging system within the VDR complements this. The alert
thread is not just a document drop — it is a structured communication
layer where messages are parsed alongside documents. A syndicate banker
typing "we are increasing the deal size to EUR 750m" in the thread is
treated as a data update, not just a note. The AI reads it, maps it to
the size field, and updates the record accordingly.

Outlook integration is offered as an optional channel for banks that
prefer to continue using email internally — messages sent from
designated syndicate email addresses can sync into the VDR thread
automatically, meaning the bank does not even need to change its
outward-facing workflow at all.

iv) Flexible, Usage-Based Pricing — Accessible to All Bank Sizes

Pricing is structured to reflect the reality that bond issuance activity
varies enormously across institutions:

- A tier-one global bank may price dozens of deals per week across
  multiple currencies and formats

- A smaller regional or national bank may bring only a handful of deals
  per year to international markets

A flat subscription model unfairly burdens smaller participants and
creates a barrier to adoption that reduces the network's value for
everyone. The New Issue VDR instead offers:

- Pay-per-deal pricing: a fee is charged per new issue processed through
  the platform, with volume discounts at defined thresholds

- AI-metered usage: computational costs scale with actual usage, passed
  through transparently

- Tiered access levels: basic alert dissemination at low cost; full
  structured data extraction, API access, and analytics at higher tiers

For a smaller bank doing ten international deals per year, the cost is
genuinely modest — far below the cost of a Bloomberg terminal licence,
and requiring no additional headcount. For a large bank, the cost is
offset almost immediately by the elimination of manual data entry and
the reduction in compliance risk.

v) Compliance — Bank-Hosted, Bank-Authorised, Bank-Controlled

This is arguably the most important selling point for the compliance and
legal functions at syndicate banks, and it directly addresses the single
greatest institutional resistance to any new fintech product in capital
markets.

The New Issue VDR is hosted and authorised by the banks themselves. This
means:

- Data sovereignty: The bank controls where its term sheets and deal
  data reside. Nothing is processed on infrastructure the bank has not
  approved.

- Retractability: Unlike Outlook, documents and alerts deposited in the
  VDR can be retracted, corrected, or superseded — with a full version
  history maintained. This is critical when deal terms change
  mid-execution that may have errors that need quickly correcting.

- Permissioned access: The bank determines precisely which vendors,
  investors, or counterparties can see which documents, at what stage of
  the deal lifecycle. A preliminary term sheet can be restricted; final
  terms can be released to all.

- Audit trail: Every upload, every AI extraction, every data push to a
  vendor API is timestamped and logged. Compliance teams have a
  complete, immutable record of what was disclosed, to whom, and when.

- Regulatory alignment: As regulators in the EU, UK, and US move toward
  greater transparency requirements in primary bond markets, having a
  structured, auditable dissemination system that maps to ICMA taxonomy
  positions the bank well ahead of likely mandatory requirements.

- MiFID II / MAR considerations: The controlled environment of the VDR
  supports compliance with market abuse regulations by ensuring that
  price-sensitive information (new issue alerts, deal terms) is
  disseminated in a controlled, permissioned, and logged manner — not
  broadcast indiscriminately via email.

The bank is not adopting a third-party dissemination service it does not
control. It is operating its own authorised infrastructure, with AI
embedded as a processing layer within that infrastructure.

The Competitive Position in One Sentence

The New Issue VDR is the first purpose-built, AI-native, ICMA-compliant
primary bond market data platform that automates dissemination and
structured data extraction at source, replacing the fragmented,
irreversible, costly patchwork of Outlook, Bloomberg, and manual vendor
rekeying — at a price accessible to every bank in the market, with
compliance controls owned by the banks themselves.
